# Pilot Readiness Map

## Purpose

Convert an opportunity into a pilot that can be executed without pretending it is already a proven program.

This skill is designed for health, digital health, AI, accessibility, voice, patient communication, and institutional innovation pilots.

## Core principle

Fast but narrow.

The goal is not a complete product. The goal is a responsible pilot with clear scope, patient protection, useful evidence, and honest claims.

## Required inputs

Useful inputs:
- Proposed technology or intervention.
- Patient or user group.
- Institutional setting.
- Partner organization.
- Desired date or milestone.
- Known risks.
- Available team.

If details are missing, propose a minimal safe version and mark assumptions.

## Method

1. Define pilot question.
   Write one primary question.
   Example:
   "Can this tool improve practical communication support for a small group of Chilean Spanish-speaking oncology patients with altered speech?"

2. Define pilot type.
   Choose one:
   - Feasibility pilot.
   - Usability pilot.
   - Technical validation.
   - Workflow validation.
   - Patient experience exploration.
   - Pre-research demonstration.

3. Define population.
   Specify:
   - Inclusion criteria.
   - Exclusion criteria.
   - Recruitment source.
   - Vulnerability considerations.
   - Consent requirements.

4. Define workflow.
   Map the minimum patient/team journey:
   Identify → invite → consent → collect baseline → use tool → collect feedback → review → report.

5. Define governance.
   At minimum:
   - Clinical owner.
   - Operational owner.
   - Technical owner.
   - Ethics/data owner.
   - Partner contact.
   - Communications owner, if public presentation is possible.

6. Define evidence.
   Evidence must match the pilot type.
   Avoid overclaiming clinical efficacy if only feasibility/usability was tested.

7. Define metrics.
   Use a small set:
   - Feasibility: number invited, number consenting, completion rate.
   - Usability: patient/team feedback, task success.
   - Technical: recognition errors, failure modes, latency, language/accent issues.
   - Safety/governance: incidents, withdrawals, data issues.
   - Narrative: patient-perceived dignity, autonomy, communication usefulness.

8. Define claims boundaries.
   State what can and cannot be said after the pilot.

## Output format

### Veredicto

State whether the pilot is viable, premature, or unsafe as described.

### Pregunta piloto

One primary pilot question.

### Tipo de piloto

Choose and justify.

### Alcance mínimo

Participants, setting, tool, duration, and workflow.

### Criterios de inclusión/exclusión

Keep narrow.

### Gobernanza mínima

Table:
Area | Owner role | Responsibility | Blocking risk

### Métricas

Table:
Metric | Why it matters | How to collect | Minimum acceptable signal

### Evidencia presentable

What can be shown at a conference or internal meeting.

### Límites de claims

What must not be claimed.

### Próximos 7–14 días

Concrete actions only.

## Quality gates

Before finalizing, verify:
- The pilot does not require a full product to start.
- Consent and data governance are not treated as afterthoughts.
- The output distinguishes feasibility from efficacy.
- Patient vulnerability is addressed.
- The next step can actually be executed by a small team.

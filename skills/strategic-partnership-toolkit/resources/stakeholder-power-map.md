# Stakeholder Power Map

## Purpose

Map people and groups around a strategic initiative so the next move is politically realistic.

This skill is not for flattering stakeholders. It is for understanding power, incentives, risk, and message fit.

## Required inputs

Useful inputs include:
- Project or decision being pursued.
- List of known stakeholders.
- Institutional setting.
- Known allies or blockers.
- Upcoming meeting, presentation, or decision point.

If the user has private impressions about informal power, treat them as internal hypotheses, not public facts.

## Method

1. Define the decision.
   - What decision needs to happen?
   - Who formally owns it?
   - Who can influence it informally?
   - Who must not be surprised?

2. Classify stakeholders.
   Use these categories:
   - Formal decision maker.
   - Informal power holder.
   - Operational owner.
   - Technical owner.
   - Clinical owner.
   - Ethical/legal gatekeeper.
   - Communications/reputation owner.
   - External partner.
   - Patient/community representative.

3. Map each stakeholder.
   Assess:
   - Power: High / Medium / Low.
   - Interest: High / Medium / Low.
   - Alignment: Ally / Neutral / Unknown / Risk / Blocker.
   - Main concern.
   - What message will matter to them.
   - What not to say to them.

4. Detect political risks.
   - Bypassing formal authority.
   - Ignoring informal authority.
   - Making technology sound like the main point.
   - Creating workload without ownership.
   - Moving faster than ethics/IT/legal can tolerate.
   - Public communication before internal alignment.

5. Build a message map.
   Each stakeholder gets:
   - One core message.
   - One proof point.
   - One ask.
   - One risk control.

6. Define sequencing.
   Not everyone should be contacted at once.
   Recommend an order:
   - Align inner cell.
   - Secure gatekeepers.
   - Brief influencers.
   - Prepare decision memo.
   - Expand to wider group.

## Two-layer model

Every stakeholder map must have two layers:

### Layer 1: Formal (for committee)

The institutional face. Suitable for presentations, memos, and committee reviews.

| Actor | Formal role | Authority | Declared interest | Position |
|-------|-----------|-----------|------------------|----------|

Use only verifiable positions. No speculation about hidden motives.

### Layer 2: Informal (political reality)

What actually drives decisions. Not for public documents.

| Actor | Real role | Leverage | Hidden interest | Risk if misread |
|-------|---------|---------|---------------|-------------------|

This layer captures:
- Who actually decides vs. who formally decides.
- Who blocks silently if not consulted early.
- Who champions privately but won't say so publicly.
- What pressures exist that aren't in the org chart.
- What happens if someone feels bypassed.

**Example:** A committee ethics board formally reviews protocols. Informally, if you present the project as a "done deal," they will reject it on principle. If you involve them early as advisors, they become allies.

**Rule:** If layer 2 contradicts layer 1, the informal reality usually wins. Design your strategy around layer 2.

## Output format

### Veredicto político

State the political reality plainly.

### Layer 1: Formal map

Table:
Actor | Role | Authority | Interest | Position | Evidence

### Layer 2: Informal map

Table:
Actor | Real role | Leverage | Hidden interest | Risk if misread

### Riesgos de manejo

List the top risks and how to avoid them.

### Secuencia recomendada

Step-by-step order of engagement.

### Mensajes por actor

Use concise, tailored language.

### Próximo movimiento

One meeting, one memo, or one alignment action.

## Quality gates

Before finalizing, verify:
- The map distinguishes formal power from informal power.
- The plan does not expose private political judgments in public wording.
- Each actor has a tailored message, not a generic pitch.
- The next step reduces risk rather than creating noise.
